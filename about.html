About

I started in a social studies classroom, which is still the hardest design brief I have ever been given: thirty people, one room, and a subject they did not choose.

Everything I do now is a version of that problem. I was the teacher making the PowerPoint do something it was not built to do, turning a lecture into a thing people could argue with. That turned into curriculum design, then into writing books and doing research, then into learning design.

I trained as an anthropologist, and it shapes how I work more than any instructional design model does. Every culture has solved the problem of passing on what it knows, and most of them solved it without a learning management system. I find that clarifying. When a brief arrives with AI already in it, the useful question is not what the technology can do — it is which part of the old human problem it is actually helping with.

I travel when I can. Looking at how other people do things is not a hobby separate from the work; it is most of the method.

The path

    Social studies teacher

    Where the interest in how people actually learn started, and where it stopped being theoretical.

  2. Next

  3. Alongside

    The lens underneath everything else: culture, how groups transmit knowledge, why some ways of teaching travel and others do not.

  4. Now
    Learning designer, Curio Group

    Storyboards, video scripts, workflow automation and AI-powered learning for universities, organisations, government partners and skills providers.
