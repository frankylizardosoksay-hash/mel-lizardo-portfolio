The problem

Start with the situation, not the solution. What was happening before? What was not working, and how did anyone know? A number here is worth three adjectives.

What we were really solving

The brief and the problem are rarely the same thing. This is where you show the reframing — the moment the conversation moved from "we need a course" to the thing that was actually wrong.

A line from a client, an SME or a learner that captures the turn. Optional, but it does a lot of work.

The design

What you made and why it took that shape. Name the decisions a reader could disagree with — those are the ones that show judgement.

  • A decision, and the reason behind it.
  • A constraint you designed around rather than complained about.
  • Something you deliberately left out.

What it did

Results, honestly stated. Completion rates, assessment data, what changed on the ground, or what people said. If you do not have numbers, say what you observed and label it as such. An honest "we never measured it" reads better than a vague claim.

What I would change

Optional, and the section most portfolios skip. It is the most convincing thing on the page.
